## Location of this UNESCO WHS site

The Taj Mahal is a **mausoleum** built on the southern (right) bank of the **Yamuna (Jamuna) River** in ***Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India***. Agra Fort, another UNESCO World Heritage Site, is also located on the right bank of the Yamuna River, about **1 mile (1.6 km)** from the Taj Mahal.

## Commissioned by

{{< history-figure src="images/history/tajmahal/shahjahan.webp" alt="Shah Jahan" caption="Shah Jahan" side="right" width="20%">}}

The Taj Mahal was commissioned by the fifth Mughal emperor, ***Shah Jahan***, in *1631*. Its construction was completed in *1648*. The project employed more than **20,000 workers and artisans** under a board of architects led by *Ustad Ahmad Lahori*, the chief architect of the emperor's court.

## Designation as a UNESCO WHS

*The Taj Mahal was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983* for being "the jewel of Islamic art in India and one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage." It is regarded as the zenith of Mughal architecture and an integral part of India's rich cultural heritage.

## Etymology

The name **"Taj Mahal"** is of Urdu origin and is believed to be derived from Arabic and Persian, with the words *tāj* and *mahall* meaning **"crown"** (*tāj*) and **"palace"** (*mahall*), respectively.

## Why was it built

{{< history-figure src="images/history/tajmahal/mumtazmahal.webp" alt="Mumtaz Mahal" caption="Mumtaz Mahal" side="right" width="25%">}}

Shah Jahan commissioned this **magnificent** monument in the **loving** memory of his wife, ***Mumtaz Mahal***, who died while giving birth to their **14th child**. She was his chief consort, and the Taj Mahal was intended to be her final resting place.

## Architecture

The Taj Mahal was designed in the Indo-Islamic and Mughal architectural styles. It is believed to have been inspired by the mausoleum of the conqueror *Timur*, ***[Gur-e Amir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur-e-Amir)*** (*Gūr-i Amīr*), in *Samarkand, Uzbekistan*. Timur was an ancestor of the Mughals and the paternal great-great-great-grandfather of Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire.

The main structure is built entirely of white marble and stands on a raised square platform. The walls are adorned with Qur'anic calligraphy and intricate floral patterns inlaid with **semi-precious** stones imported from across the world.

Entry to the Taj Mahal complex is through a monumental gate built of richly decorated red sandstone, creating a **grand first impression**. Four minarets flank the mausoleum, each intentionally tilted slightly outward so that, in the event of a collapse, they would fall away from the main tomb.

The **false sarcophagi** (cenotaphs) of Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan are located in the central chamber, while their actual tombs lie in the crypt below. Mumtaz Mahal's cenotaph occupies the exact center of the chamber on an ornate marble base. The cenotaphs are aligned north–south, with their heads pointing north. According to Islamic burial tradition, the bodies were laid on their right sides with their faces turned westward toward Mecca.

## Some Interesting Myths and Facts

1. Legend has it that Shah Jahan intended to build a **Black Taj Mahal** for himself on the opposite bank of the Yamuna River. However, his plans were interrupted when he was imprisoned by his son, Aurangzeb. Modern research suggests that this story is likely a myth.

2. Due to the reflective properties of white marble, the Taj Mahal appears to change color throughout the day—pinkish at sunrise, brilliant white in daylight, and golden under moonlight.

3. A popular legend claims that Shah Jahan ordered the hands of the artisans to be cut off to prevent them from replicating this masterpiece. However, there is no credible historical evidence to support this story.

4. The decorative floral designs are made using pietra dura (called parchin kari in India), where tiny pieces of colored stones are inlaid into marble.

5. The Taj stands on the bank of the Yamuna, where the soil is soft. Instead of a conventional foundation, the builders used numerous masonry wells filled with stone to support the enormous weight.


6. The white marble came from Makrana, Rajasthan, the same source that is still used for restoration work today.
