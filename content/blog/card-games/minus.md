## **What is Minus?**

Minus is a strategic, four-player trick-taking game where your success depends on **predicting how many tricks (hands) you can win**. The challenge is that you bid based on incomplete information, then have to deliver on your promise.

The name "Minus" comes from the penalty: if you fail to meet your bid, you **lose points** — a negative, or "minus", score.

### **Card Rankings**

| Card | Rank |
|------|------|
| Ace (A) | Highest |
| King (K) | 2nd highest |
| Queen (Q) | 3rd highest |
| Jack (J) | 4th highest |
| 10 down to 2 | Numbered order (10 high → 2 low) |

*Ranking example: ♠A beats ♠K beats ♠10 beats ♠2 (highest to lowest within a suit).*

<strong>The Goal:</strong> The player with the highest total score wins. You gain points by meeting your bid, and you lose points if you fall short of it.

## **Important Terminologies**

{{< concept title="Bid" >}}
Your prediction of how many tricks you will win during the round.

**Example:**
- You bid 5 → you must win **at least 5** tricks
- If you win 5 or more tricks → you score **+5** points ✓
- If you win only 4 tricks → you score **−5** points (a "Minus") ✗

**Key rule:** Your final bid can never be lower than **2** in any round.
{{< /concept >}}

{{< concept title="Trump Suit" >}}
A special suit, chosen during Phase 1, that **beats every other suit**.

**Example — if Hearts is Trump:**
- Even the lowest Heart (♥2) beats any card of another suit, so ♥2 beats ♠A, ♦K and ♣Q
- Within the trump suit, normal rank still applies — ♥2 loses to a higher Heart such as ♥3 or ♥A
- A higher trump always beats a lower trump

**Important:** Trump is set during Phase 1 (the first 5 cards). The player who wins the bidding chooses the trump suit.
{{< /concept >}}

{{< concept title="Trick" >}}
One **round of play** in which each of the four players plays exactly one card.

**How a trick is won:**
1. The **highest card of the led suit** wins, **unless**
2. A **trump** is played — the highest trump then wins, beating every non-trump card

**Example (Hearts is Trump):**
- Player 1 leads ♠7
- Player 2 plays ♠J
- Player 3 plays ♥5 (a Trump)
- Player 4 plays ♦K

**Player 3 wins** — they played a Trump, which beats all other suits.
{{< /concept >}}

{{< concept title="The Lead" >}}
Being the first player to play a card in a new trick.

**Rules of the lead:**
- The player with the **highest bid** leads the first trick
- The player who **won the previous trick** leads the next one
- Once a card is led, the other players **must follow suit** (play the same suit) if they can
- If you can follow suit, you must also play a **higher card of that suit when you have one**
{{< /concept >}}

## **Dealing Phases (5-4-4)**

The 13 cards each player receives are dealt in **three distinct stages**:

{{< concept title="Opening Hand" >}}
- Each player is dealt **5 cards**
- This is where **Trump is set**
- **Bidding happens now:**
  - Going in turn, each player either **bids at least 5** or **passes**
  - The **highest bidder wins the auction** and chooses the Trump suit
  - Example: if you win with a bid of 6 and name Hearts, every Heart is now Trump
- If **no one bids 5 or more**, Trump defaults to **Spades**
- Phase 2 cannot begin until Trump is set
{{< /concept >}}

**Example auction:**
- Player A: passes
- Player B: bids 5
- Player C: bids 6
- Player D: passes

→ **Player C wins and sets Trump** (the highest bid).

{{< concept title="Second Deal" >}}
- Each player receives **4 more cards**, for **9 cards** in total
- Trump is now fixed for the round
- The player who won the auction is **committed to at least their Phase 1 bid** and cannot lower it
- Players use these new cards to reassess their hand, but no new formal bids are made yet
{{< /concept >}}

{{< concept title="Final Deal" >}}
- Each player receives the **final 4 cards**, for **13 cards** in total
- Each player now announces their **final bid (2–13)** — this is your binding commitment for the round
- The player who set Trump announces it once more to confirm
- Play then begins, led by the highest bidder
{{< /concept >}}

## **Playing the Tricks**

Once all 13 cards are dealt and final bids are locked, the round is played out as **13 tricks**:

1. The highest bidder **leads** the first trick by playing any card.
2. Going in turn, every other player **follows suit** if they can — and plays a higher card of that suit when they hold one.
3. A player with no card of the led suit may play any card, including a **Trump**.
4. The trick is won by the **highest Trump played**, or — if no Trump was played — by the **highest card of the led suit**.
5. The winner of the trick **leads** the next one.

This continues until all 13 cards are played, after which each player counts the number of tricks they won.

## **Scoring**

At the end of every round, compare each player's **tricks won** against their **final bid**:

- **Bid met or exceeded** → score **+ (your bid)** points
- **Bid not met** → score **− (your bid)** points

*Example: you bid 5. If you win 5 or more tricks, you score +5. If you win 4 or fewer, you score −5.*

Notice that your score is always tied to the **bid you committed to**, not the exact number of tricks you won — so over-bidding is risky, and under-bidding wastes a strong hand.

## **Winning the Game**

- Scores are tracked across multiple rounds.
- The player with the **highest total score** at the end is the winner.


## **Final Thoughts**
 
Minus rewards careful judgement over luck. Because you commit to a bid before seeing your full hand, every decision — how aggressively to bid in Phase 1, which suit to fight for as Trump, when to spend a high card — carries real weight. Bid too high and the "minus" will punish you; bid too low and you leave points on the table.
 
It's a game of nerve and counting, best enjoyed with four players who don't mind a little friendly risk-taking. One bold bid can swing an entire round.
