## **What is War?**

War is the simplest card game in existence. Two players compete by flipping cards, and the player with the higher card wins the round. The entire game is determined by luck — no strategy, no decisions, just pure chance. It's perfect for young children learning about card rankings.

### **Card Rankings**

| Card | Rank |
|------|------|
| Ace (A) | Highest |
| King (K) | Second Highest |
| Queen (Q) | Third Highest |
| Jack (J) | Fourth Highest |
| 10-2 | Numbered value |
| 2 | Lowest |

*Example: ♠A beats ♥K beats ♦10 beats ♣2*

<div class="highlight-rule">
<strong>Setup:</strong> Shuffle the deck thoroughly and deal all 52 cards face-down equally to each player. Each player receives 26 cards in a stack without looking at them.
</div>

**Objective:** Win all 52 cards by having the highest card in each round.


## **Important Terminologies**

{{< concept title="Battle" >}}
A single round where both players simultaneously reveal one card from the top of their deck.

The player with the **higher-ranked card** wins and takes both cards, placing them at the bottom of their deck.

Card order (highest to lowest): Ace > King > Queen > Jack > 10 > 9 > 8 > 7 > 6 > 5 > 4 > 3 > 2
{{< /concept >}}

**Important:**
- No choices to make — you must play your top card every round
- The ranking is fixed and never changes
- Speed is irrelevant — simultaneously flip your card


{{< concept title="War" >}}
When both players reveal **cards of equal rank**, a **War** begins:

1. Each player places **3 cards face-down** on the table (without looking)
2. Each player reveals a **4th card face-up**
3. The player with the **higher 4th card** wins all 10 cards
4. If the 4th cards tie again, repeat the war process

The winner takes all cards and places them at the bottom of their deck.
{{< /concept >}}

## **Turn Structure**

On your turn, you **must do exactly one thing:**

{{< concept title="Reveal & Battle" >}}
1. Both players simultaneously hold their deck face-down
2. Each player flips their top card face-up
3. Compare the two revealed cards
4. The player with the higher-ranked card wins both cards
5. Winner places both cards at the bottom of their deck

Repeat this process until someone wins all 52 cards.
{{< /concept >}}

## **Wars and Tied Cards**

{{< concept title="When Cards Match" >}}
If both players reveal cards of the **same rank**, a **War** is triggered:

Each player places 3 cards face-down, then reveals a 4th card. The higher 4th card wins all cards in the pile.

If those 4th cards also tie, the war repeats until someone wins.
{{< /concept >}}

Your opponent can then take the cards. If you run out of cards during a war, you automatically lose.

**No scoring system:** War has no points. You simply collect cards and keep playing until one player owns all 52 cards.


## **Card Rankings**

The ranking system never changes and is absolute. Higher cards always beat lower cards. There are no exceptions and no strategic way around it.

## **Winning the Game**

**Victory conditions:**
- A player wins when they own all **52 cards**
- The losing player has **zero cards** and cannot continue
- **Game length:** Most games last 5–30 minutes, though closely matched decks can extend to an hour or more

## **Final Thoughts**

War is often dismissed as being purely luck-based — and that criticism is fair. There is no strategy, no decision-making, and no skill involved. But that's exactly why it's valuable for young children. It teaches card rankings, number comparison, and the fundamental structure of card games: reveal, compare, win, collect.

The rhythm of War is meditative and simple. There's no tension, no pressure, just the pure chance of the next flip. Wars create moments of excitement when the outcome suddenly shifts. War proves that games don't need complexity to be entertaining.
