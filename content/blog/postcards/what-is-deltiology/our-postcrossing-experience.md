## What is postcrossing?

By Wikipedia definition; postcrossing is an online project for people to exchange postcards with other project members globally. The project's tag line is "Send a postcard and receive a postcard back from a random person somewhere in the world!"

However, I think it is more than that. The very fact that there are people taking time from their everyday schedules to carefully write addresses, tid-bits about random stuff, sticking stamps, and dropping them in letterboxes is a wholesome thing. Majority of the people doing it are in their late 50s; and it is beautiful to see this action stay alive.


## Is postcrossing nice?
It certainly depends on your taste for sure. Many of our friends love observing the entire process when we stick beautiful stamps that we buy from the Philatelic Bureau of various post offices, write addresses with such varied pin codes, sometimes get beautiful and unique permanent pictorial cancellations, and drop them in the bright red post boxes. But they admit they would not be able to do it.

{{< img src="images/postcards/deltiology/shimla-postoffice.webp" alt="Shimla Post Office" caption="Shimla Post Office" >}}


The hobby does get a bit expensive because we fork out money to purchase postcards, stamps, and try to sometimes even get our trip photos printed in postcard quality paper (approximately 350–450 GSM). However, there is something oddly satisfying about this. I personally find it poetic: warm hands writing a short message across a small 14 cm× 9 cm paper, sticking stamps imagining the olden times of people communicating only in this way, writing addresses carefully as a single mistake can get the postcard lost. Your thoughts travel across space and time to reach someone else who value this act as much as you do.


The art of taking efforts to write for communication is dwindling. People have texts, blogs, voice notes, podcasts, emails, etc. etc. Letters, postcards are beautiful things of the past that should be kept alive in our generation for its simple charm.

This hobby gives us immense happiness. I've written about an example of such an incident below.

Someone had asked us to write to their grandfather to cheer him up during COVID-19 lockdown. We did, and they then sent the cutest picture to us of him receiving the postcard.


Another wholesome incident was that one of our friends got a grand-daughter after ages. She sent a postcard about her, along with a footprint!

{{< img src="images/postcards/deltiology/izzy-card.webp" >}}


While sending digital photos make a person feel connected, I feel receiving a tangible memento of someone is a beautiful keepsake. Our ancient ancestors kept tangible records of everything; maybe it is inbuilt within us, maybe it feels natural. Postcards are the sweetest way to connect with people without any hesitance. I think of it like this; it is a piece of someone from somewhere travelling miles and miles for you to hold it in your hand and a tiny golden thread of connection builds across the world.
