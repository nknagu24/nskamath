Ultrafast TA spectroscopy is an advanced characterization technique which employs ultrashort laser pulses typically in the range of few femtoseconds (fs) to explore the transient optical properties in a system. The change in absorbance is monitored over a time range which provides the dynamics of the observed species after photoexcitation. In this section, detailed overview of this technique will be discussed.

## Working Principle of TA spectroscopy
This is a pump-probe technique where an excitation (pump) pulse excites the carriers from the ground state to excited state then a probe pulse is incident immediately after excitation. The absorption of the system is measured at different interval of time after photoexcitation and this is controlled by a delay stage that introduces a time delay between pump and probe pulses. We measure the change in absorption ($\Delta{A}$) as a function of time ($t$) this provides us with the dynamics of the excited state population of the material. 

## Instrumentation 

The detailed schematic of the TA spectroscopy is provided in Figure XX. In this setup a regenerative amplifier (Spectra Physics – Spitfire Ace) is used as the source of fs pulses. This amplifier was seeded by a Ti:Sapphire, 800 nm oscillator (Mai Tai SP, Spectra Physics) which provides a ~35-fs pulse as output. To amplify its output a continuous wave (CW) pump laser high-power, Q-switched diode-pumped solid-state (DPSS) green laser operating at 527 nm (frequency-doubled Nd:YLF). Using a Ti:Sapphire gain medium the output of 800 nm, 4 mJ pulse energy is obtained at a repetition rate of 1 kHz. This output is then divided as required and is used to generate pump and probe pulses used to perform TA spectroscopy. To obtain specified wavelengths for pump and probe pulses a optical parametric amplifier (TOPAS) is used which generates a monochromatic output of range, 250 nm to 1600 nm.

In our setup we used two TOPAS individually for pump and probes this is mainly used to measure decay kinetics of a specific state. In order to obtain a broadband spectra, a white light continuum is generated by splitting a fraction of 800 nm output from the amplifier and is focused on sapphire crystal. This is then used as a probe light in the TA measurements. The repetition rate of pump is halved to 500 Hz by using a mechanical chopper. This is done so that the absorption can be measured in with pump and without pump conditions. The time delay between pump and probe is controlled using a delay line where the translation of the stage corresponds to a particular interval of time with respect to the pump pulse. Probe beam is split into two parts before reaching the sample, one half is used as a reference beam. The pump and probe beams are then focused tightly onto the sample and the pump light is discarded taking the probe light on to the detector. detector used here depends on the measurement involved. For measurement of single wavelength kinetics two photo diodes of variable gain (Pascher Instrument) were used. For broadband absorption measurements the spectra were recorded using a CCD array equipped with a grating spectrograph (Acton spectra Pro SP 2300).
