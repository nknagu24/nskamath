Himachal Pradesh is almost synonymous with Kullu-Manali. For us, Manali is just four hours away. Manali was just a small village near the border of the Kullu district and the Lahaul district. The Pir-Panjal Range (further moves east to form part of the Greater Himalayas) lies at this border. It contains the famous Rohtang Pass which was previously used to travel to Lahaul. Now, the Atal Tunnel; a 9km long horseshoe shaped tunnel connects the two. It also is a marvel, as it was built under a whole mountain.

Manali is breathtaking: both in the summers and the winters. The quaint town has now developed a lot because of tourism. The famous sights are the Mall Road, Hidimba Devi Temple, Manu Temple, Vashisht Temple, Old Manali and Jogini Waterfalls. We've visited all of them, and each sight has a quiet charm to it.

While these are the places with maximum tourists who look to eat, roam and hang out, the true natural beauty lies in the main temple and the scenic sights.

Jogini Waterfalls requires a little trek, but it is extremely easy and the viewpoint is beautiful. We encountered a lot of tourists then, and the experience was really pleasant. 

A friend of ours said that the forests behind the Hidimba Devi Temple looked like an absolute Narnia forest. Indeed, the tall deodar trees give us a very solemn vibe with absolute peace. The whole of Himachal has a very quiet, spiritual vibe as this was fabled to be the place the Pandavas from the Mahabharata spent time in. This temple is dedicated to one of the wives of Bhima and is of demoness origin. She was kind, powerful and revered along with her son Ghatotkach who also has a shrine dedicated to him, opposite of her temple. While the temple now has a tourist-y vibe attached to it, at the core of it, the spiritual energy resonates.

This temple is so famous that the Permanent Pictorial Cancellation of this place is the Hidimba Temple. The funny story is that the Post Office people do not know much of this. We tried twice to get a good collection; the first time he didn't do it well at all and the second time, we asked assertively and they took ages searching for the materials. 

The place is an absolute pleasure to be in to get away from the hustle and bustle of daily life. The river Beas runs through here and some hotels are set up on the banks of it. 

This is a random structure. But most of the temples here are beautiful. The Vashisht temple has a natural hot spring and is said to contain healing waters. The Manu temple is said to be dedicated to the first human who descended in the Himalayas after the great flood. There is a Buddhist monastery too, with the prayer wheels that we can rotate. It is said that rotating them is equivalent to one mantra.

From Manali, to often get snow, people travel to Solang Valley. However, for sure snow, people travel through this famous tunnel and enter Lahaul. This is a beautiful tunnel, constructed by the Border Roads Organization (BRO) and it is drivers' paradise to drive in this smooth tunnel. 

Kullu-Manali under snow is winter wonderland. The purity and serenity of the scenery will calm any soul. The people are simple, the weather pollution-free and the land has some energy that refreshes you. I believe it is the piety of the people to nature and the Gods that gives the place a holy aura.

The religion predominant here is Hinduism and Buddhism, however the Mall Road comes alive during Christmas and New Year. A sense of calm pervades the area despite the tourists and rush. Manali is a beautiful must-visit place to gather a sense of the spirit of Himachal Pradesh. 

We will conclude with a cute fact. We wondered why Atal Vajpayee was associated with this place, so on checking, we found out that he loved a village near Manali called Prini. He was a fan of the trout that was prepared here, the tribals from Lahaul-Spiti were close associates of him and the people loved him. But the cute part is that he had a close female friend Mrs. Rajkumari Kaul whose daughter he later adopted. 

Political scientist Vinay Sitapati, in his book 'Jugalbandi: The BJP before Modi', writes "The heart of the relationship between Vajpayee and Rajkumari was intellectual. From a provincial north Indian milieu, Vajpayee was both perplexed by as well as attracted to an educated woman who could hold her own."

She married someone else, a Philosophy professor, but the couple remained great friends of Atal Vajpayee. We discussed this sitting on the benches in the Mall Road staring at his immense statue there. A thrill came over me, the beauty of the story and his statue transported me back to a time where they possibly talked to each other, had discussions and felt comfort in each other's company.

"'Vajpayeeji, can you tell us about Mrs Kaul?"

Unfazed, Vajpayee thought for a while, then replied with smiling eyes, "Kashmir jaisa maamla hai."
Sagarika Ghose's Atal Bihari Vajpayee

And she was a Kashmiri Pandit.

And we digress. Manali "Manu-alaya" "Abode of Manu" is a quintessential Himachali town that people have to visit at least once in their lifetime.
