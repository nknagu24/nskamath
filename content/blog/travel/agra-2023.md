So much buildup! We were also going at the end of December; a time of smog and mist. Would we feel the true beauty of the Taj Mahal? We had to find out.

We had booked online tickets, and we entered the grounds through the West Gate at 6am. The morning was misty and cold and the air dense with curiosity and expectation.

This itself was taking our breath away. The sandstone architecture, the intricate designs and we late found out that the Urdu text was inscribed such that the top parts were enlarged to negate the effect of narrowing perspective. Hence, the inscription looks uniform when you look at it from down. The black marble lettering is inlaid into white marble scroll-like borders that frame the architectural features. Sweeping letters and a strong emphasis on horizontal and vertical strokes create an almost grid-like effect in places. The math, the designing and the thought processes behind the details were really fascinating.

Then came the VIP sight. The white beauty. We forgot everything for a few seconds as we stared at it. The rush of emotions was of a] wonder because we are finally beholding something that we have just seen in print in textbooks, internet, etc. and b] the sheer, pure beauty and the serenity it washes over you. It feels unreal, like something that legit fell from heaven. It shouldn't exist in our world. The beauty is unreal, sublime and just unbelievable.

We whipped out our InstaX and captured it in a photo of tangible form, hoping our memory and emotions of seeing it also get captured in the photo for a lifetime.


Every step we take towards it, we just get blown away. The beauty doesn't get old, the monument keeps on wow-ing us, the fascination and marvelling doesn't stop. We enter in and stand in silence observing the two tombs: a central tomb for Mumtaz Mahal and a tomb next to it of Shah Jahan. We understand that he has accomplished what he wanted to do for his wife: create an everlasting mausoleum for her; an embodiment of his love for her.

He has made a monument of perfect Indo-Islamic fusion architecture. The detail that went in its design was by the head architect Ustad-Ahmad Lahori and people from India, Iran and Central Asia were commissioned to work on it. The architectural elements like the rhythmic combination of solids and voids, concave and convex and light shadow; such as arches and domes further increases the aesthetic aspect. The pietra dura is unreal almost. The minarets built with a slight outward angle to protect the monument is a testament to how intricately every aspect was designed. I think a whole book can be written about the design and historical aspect of the Taj Mahal.

The Char Bagh (Four Gardens) starts from the end of the main gateway and ends near the square base of the mausoleum. Its magnificent scale elaborates on the concept of a garden in paradise. A paradise that, according to Islamic beliefs, consists of four rivers: one of water, one of milk, one of honey, and one of wine. And it is from this concept that Char Bagh of the Taj Mahal originated. Since four is considered the holiest number in Islam, all the arrangements of the Charbagh Garden of the Taj Mahal are based on four or its multiples. The entire garden is divided into four parts, with two marble canals studded with fountains crossing in the center. In each quarter portion, 16 flowerbeds have been divided by stone-paved raised pathways. It is said that even each of the flowerbeds was planted with 400 plants. At the center of the garden, between the Taj and its gateway, is a raised marble lotus tank with a cusped border, which reflects the Taj in its waters. The four walkways are identical but differentiated through their context

The Taj Mahal is a perfect symmetrical planned building, with an emphasis of bilateral symmetry along a central axis on which the main features are placed. The building material used is brick-in-lime mortar veneered with red sandstone and marble and inlay work of precious/semi precious stones. The mosque and the guest house in the Taj Mahal complex are built of red sandstone in contrast to the marble tomb in the centre. Both the buildings have a large platform over the terrace at their front. Both the mosque and the guest house are the identical structures. They have an oblong massive prayer hall consist of three vaulted bays arranged in a row with central dominant portal. The frame of the portal arches and the spandrels are veneered in white marble. The spandrels are filled with flowery arabesques of stone intarsia and the arches bordered with rope molding.

An associate of mine said that she said the Taj Mahal when it was affected by pollution changing its colour. She said it was so apparent as restoration work was happening at that time and the before-after colour difference was so stark. Hence to protect its colour, an area of 10,400 sq km around the Taj Mahal is defined to protect the monument from pollution. The Supreme Court of India in December, 1996, delivered a ruling banning use of coal/coke in industries located in the Taj Trapezium Zone (TTZ) and switching over to natural gas or relocating them outside the TTZ. The TTZ comprises of 40 protected monuments including three World Heritage Sites - Taj Mahal, Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri.

The dome is more than 40 m high and 4 m thick, yet it seems to float over the marble façade. To make it visible as a perfect dome, a raise is given which is seen only by air. The dome is self-supporting, with no reinforcing struts or columns. The weight of the dome is transferred directly downwards to the massive masonry below.

I personally think a visit to the Taj Mahal is a bit life-changing. It is a prime example of why beauty is important. I am a science student, a biologist in fact, and the concept of psychological changes that happen when you gaze on art or beauty is extremely interesting. My physicist friend however, finds the symmetry and the perfect dimensions of the design fascinating. Art has something in it that curiously invokes some unnamed emotion in humans. The viewing of the Taj Mahal is a prime example of this. A must-visit for everyone for sure.
